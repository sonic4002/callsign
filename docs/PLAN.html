Callsign · אות קריאה

A browser real-time strategy game where every squad is a real AI agent. You don't click units. You talk to your operator, and your army figures out how to win.

Alpha

Concept

Think C&C Generals meets EndWar, played over a real military radio net. EndWar let you speak fixed phrases to your units. Callsign lets you speak freely, because your commander is an LLM that understands intent and breaks it into orders for its squads.

Core loop

  1. Capture oil rigs on land and at sea; they pump gold automatically.
  2. Spend on new squads or smarter brains.
  3. Give orders over the radio, by voice or text.
  4. Squads plan, fireteams fight, you adapt.
  5. Destroy the enemy HQ or hold more points at the timer.

Design pillars

  • Quick: matches of 10–15 minutes, playable on a phone.
  • Command, not clicks: skill is in strategy and how well you brief your operator.
  • Fair: brains are earned with in-game gold, never bought with money.
  • Alive: units react to fatigue, weather and sight on their own.
Bravo

Chain of command

Four layers, built like a real army. Only the middle two use LLMs, which keeps cost and latency sane even with hundreds of units on the map.

YouSupreme commander
Human

Give intent by voice or text from your phone. Watch the map, decide what to buy.

OperatorGeneral · 1 per player
LLM · strong model

Talks with you, understands intent, keeps the big picture, and turns "hold Foxtrot" into tasks for squads. Callsign: Anvil.

Squad leaderCaptain · 1 agent each
LLM · tier-based

Runs as its own agent in the player's browser. Wakes on events (new order, contact, losses), not every tick. Decides formation, routes, when to fall back.

FireteamsSoldiers · 2–4 per team
Utility AI · no LLM

Plain game code on the server. Each team scores its options every tick from local conditions and acts at once. Free and instant.

Buying an agent means buying a new squad leader, which starts as a new agent in your browser. A squad of 8–12 units costs one agent, so a big army is a few dozen agents, not hundreds.
Charlie

Field autonomy

Mission command, IDF style: the commander gives the goal and the intent, the team on the ground decides how. A pair in an ambush that spots the enemy doesn't wait for permission.

What a fireteam reads

  • Nerve: fear, suppression and losses in one value, shown only through behavior and radio, never as a bar
  • Ammo and health
  • Line of sight and cover
  • Local weather (rain cuts sight and accuracy)
  • Enemies seen, and how close
  • Current order and the intent behind it
  • Personality: aggressive, careful, steady

Example: north pair, ambush at Hotel

Clear sight

+0.3

The squad leader LLM sees a summary every few seconds and adjusts: "North pair is pinned, send the south pair around the flank."

Nerve, in play

  • Steady: normal speed and accuracy.
  • Shaken: hugs cover, fires less, radio gets tense.
  • Pinned: crawls or freezes, calls for help: "We're pinned at the bridge!"
  • Recovers out of fire, near a leader, or after a win. Veterans shake less.

Dropped on purpose

  • Fatigue: a hidden bar to manage, too slow for a 10-minute match.
  • Separate morale stat: merged into nerve.
  • Nerve is tested in Phase 0. If players can't read it at a glance, it goes too.
Obedience first. Squads always carry out the order. They improvise only how they do it, never whether. Freedom lives inside your intent, never against it.

Economy, brains & superpowers

One resource: gold. Like the oil derricks in Red Alert, oil rigs on land and at sea pump oil that turns into gold on its own. There's nothing to harvest or haul. The economy is pure map control: hold rigs and you get rich, lose them and you go broke. That pushes every fight toward the rigs.

Superpowers through the build tree

Superpowers are never unlocked by the clock. Like Red Alert, you build the right chain of buildings, then pay gold for each launch. A recharge after each use stops spamming. The moment a superpower building goes up, the enemy is warned, and a race begins: destroy it, or build your own first.

FactionBuild chainBuilding costEach launch
Aegis · Orbital EyeHQ → Power plant → Radar → Satellite uplink2,500 gold1,000 gold · 4 min recharge
Ashfall · Tunnel SurgeHQ → Barracks → Tunnel network → Deep tunnel complex2,000 gold800 gold · 4 min recharge

This gives every match a natural build-up. The early game is about rigs, the middle about the build chain, and the end about who launches first. It has the tension of DEFCON, driven by your choices and not a timer.

Numbers are starting points for balancing, not final.

Echo

Fairness & who pays

Players bring their own AI key and pay for their own army's thinking. Every AI call goes through a thin relay on the game server, and the relay picks the model by the squad's brain tier. You pay for no AI, and nobody's squad thinks smarter than the gold they spent.

Main game: casual and ranked

  • Connect in one click through OpenRouter, or paste a Claude, Gemini or OpenAI key.
  • Agents never call the AI directly. They send prompts to the relay.
  • The relay checks the squad's tier on the game server, picks the model, and calls the provider with the player's key.
  • A gold upgrade switches that squad's model on its next thought.
  • Fair from day one, so ranked ships at launch.

Open league: MCP

  • The game exposes an MCP server with the same tools: radio, move, hold, attack, report, buy.
  • Connect any agent: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, your own Swarm.
  • The agent picks its own model, so tiers don't apply.
  • Separate ladder, the "whose agent is smartest" arena.
Your running cost is the game server, the relay and file hosting. The relay only forwards text, so it costs far less than running AI. A Tier 1 match should cost a player a few cents. Revenue later can come from cosmetics or a season pass, never stronger brains.

Architecture

The server runs the real-time simulation and the radio net. The player's browser runs their agents, and the relay sends every AI call to the model the squad's tier allows, billed to the player's key. A slow AI call never freezes the battle and never costs you.

What runs where

  • Game server: movement, combat, economy, fog of war, radio routing. Owns all truth. Runs no AI.
  • Player's browser: map, radio, voice, and all of that player's agents as background workers.
  • Model relay: holds the player's key encrypted, picks the model by tier, forwards the call. Runs no AI.
  • AI provider: billed to the player.
  • MCP endpoint: the same game tools for open league agents.
  • File hosting: sprites, sounds and voice phrases, made once on your machine.

Agent rules

  • Agents act only through game tools: radio, move, hold, attack, report, buy.
  • The server checks every action, so a modified browser can't add gold or units.
  • Agents see structured game state and their own side's radio, never enemy text.
  • Every major model supports this tool-call format, so one runtime serves them all.

Right-to-left interface

  • Whole HUD mirrors with dir="rtl"; CSS uses logical properties.
  • Numbers, gold counts and timers stay left-to-right inside Hebrew text.
  • Hebrew font: Heebo or Rubik, matched in weight to the English face.
  • The map itself never mirrors; only panels and text do.

Rules for content

  • No text is ever baked into images. Labels are drawn by the game, so one sprite serves both languages.
  • All strings live in he.json and en.json; a missing key fails the build.
  • All radio talk follows real procedure in both languages (see Quebec), reviewed by a native speaker who served, not machine-translated.

Oscar

What keeps players coming back

Fast, intense matches get people in. These are what bring them back for "one more game".

Veterans with memory

Squads that survive gain a rank, a name and memories of past battles. Next match, the squad leader warns: "Careful, last time we lost two men at this bridge." Power resets every match; the stories carry over. No other RTS can do this, because it needs LLMs.

Radio as the soundtrack

Hebrew and English chatter calling contacts, arguing and joking sets the mood of the whole game. Each faction has its own voice and slang. Done well, people play just to listen.

Clip-worthy moments

Agents will surprise you, like an ambush you never ordered that wins the fight. A one-tap replay shows what happened and the agent's reasoning, ready to share.

The superpower race

The warning when an enemy superpower building goes up turns every match into a race. Destroy it in time, or launch yours first. A clear peak for every game.

Comebacks

The HQ well keeps a beaten player alive, sea rigs offer a big swing, and one well-placed superpower can turn a lost game. Matches stay tense to the end.

Short and complete

10 to 15 minutes, start to finish, on a phone. Long enough for a story, short enough for "one more".

Papa

Joining & connecting

Nothing to install. A player opens a link in Chrome, Safari or Edge, on a phone or a computer, connects an AI key, and plays. The game can also be added to the home screen like an app. No Docker and no desktop service.

  1. Open the link and sign in.
  2. Connect AI: "Sign in with OpenRouter" in one click, or paste a Claude, Gemini or OpenAI key. The key is stored encrypted on the relay, used only for that player's agents, and never sent back to the browser.
  3. Create or join a room. The game shows the expected AI cost of a match.
  4. Match starts: the browser starts the operator and each squad leader as a background worker, one per agent.
  5. Agents think: each one sends its prompt to the relay with a match token. The relay looks up the squad's tier, picks the model, calls the provider with the player's key, and returns the answer.
  6. Agents act through game tools: radio, move, hold, attack, report, and buy for the operator.
  7. Upgrades: pay 300 gold and that squad's next thought goes to the Tier 2 model. Buying a squad starts a new agent; losing one stops it.

What is OpenRouter?

A service that gives one account and one key for models from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and others, paid from credit the player adds. Players can cap what the key may spend. It's only a billing route: the relay still chooses the model.

What it costs you

  • Game server: simulation and radio routing.
  • Relay: forwards text. No AI, no match state.
  • Static hosting: art, sounds and voice files from a CDN.
  • AI: zero. Each player pays on their own key.

Phones in the background

If a player switches apps mid-match, the phone pauses the page and their agents stop thinking. Squads keep their last orders and fireteams keep fighting on the server, so the army never just freezes.

Open league over MCP

The game publishes an MCP endpoint with the same tools. A player creates an open league room, gets a match token, and points any MCP agent at it: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, or Swarm. The agent brings its own model and pays its own way. Optional later: Callsign Base, a small desktop app for local models through Ollama.

Quebec

Radio net

Everything happens on the radio, using real radio procedure in Hebrew and English. There's one command net. Every message is heard by your whole side and opens with who it's for and who is talking.

Hammer → Viper 2 "Viper 2, this is Hammer. Hold Foxtrot. Over." "צפע 2, כאן פטיש, תחזיקו את פסל, עבור." Viper 2 → Hammer "Hammer, this is Viper 2. Wilco. Out." "פטיש, כאן צפע 2, רות, יבוצע, סוף."
You start withWho acts
"Viper 2, this is Hammer…" / "צפע 2, כאן פטיש…"Only Viper 2. Everyone else hears it.
"Viper 2 and Rhino 1, this is Hammer…"Both squads, and they coordinate with each other.
"All stations, this is Hammer…" / "כל התחנות, כאן פטיש…"Everyone.
"Anvil, this is Hammer…" / "סדן, כאן פטיש…"The operator, for plans, purchases and status.
No callsignThe operator decides who it's for. Without an operator, the game asks "Who is this for?"

Routing happens in game code by matching callsigns in both languages, so it's instant and needs no AI. Only the message content goes to the agents.

No callsign is a word from either phonetic alphabet, so a squad never sounds like a map point. "Eagle / נשר" and "Wolf / זאב" were dropped for that reason.

You outrank everyone

  • Call any squad directly, and your order wins over the operator's.
  • The squad confirms: "Hammer, this is Viper 2. Wilco. Out."
  • The operator hears it on the net and plans around it, never against it.
  • "Anvil, this is Hammer. Take back Viper 2. Out." hands control back.

Room options

  • With operator: you give intent, it handles the details.
  • Without operator: you command squad leaders directly. Harder.
  • Match length: 10, 15, 20 minutes, or until one HQ falls.
  • Radio language: Hebrew, English or mixed.

Channels

  • Command net: you, the operator and squad leaders. Always on.
  • Squad nets: tap a squad to listen to its fireteams.
  • Enemy net: encrypted. A later faction can jam it or listen in.

On the phone

  • A big push-to-talk button, held with the thumb.
  • Tap a squad on the map to fill in its callsign, then speak.
  • A written radio log of every message, for silent play.
